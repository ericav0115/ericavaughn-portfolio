Planning Platform Reliability & Performance

01 · The challenge

The symptom showed up in the app. The cause didn’t live there.

A production planning platform was slowing down and failing intermittently. Users saw the problem in the application, but the causes ran across Databricks processing, PostgreSQL, serving tables, scheduled jobs, migrations, and deployment.

02 · What I did

What I owned and built.

  • Traced performance and reliability issues across Databricks, Supabase/PostgreSQL, Vercel, Sentry, scheduled jobs, migrations, and serving tables.
  • Identified lock contention, severe dead-row and index bloat, and expensive rebuild patterns as the real drivers.
  • Reworked the backend and production architecture, shaping a processing design that avoids costly rebuilds and holds up under load.
  • Translated the findings for application, data, and business stakeholders so fixes could be prioritized together.

04 · Key decisions

The judgment behind the build.

Why investigate across layers?

Fixing the layer where a symptom appears often just moves the problem. Following the request path end to end is how you find what actually changed.

Why treat bloat as a design problem?

Dead rows and index bloat are usually a byproduct of how data gets written. Changing the write pattern prevents them from coming back.

Why lead with observability?

You can’t prioritize what you can’t see. Error and timing signals turned anecdotes into evidence.

06 · What’s next

Where it goes from here.

Formalize freshness and performance SLOs, add alerting on lock waits and bloat, and make serving-table maintenance part of the deployment process.
