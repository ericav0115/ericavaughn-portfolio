Cloud Identity & Deployment Automation

A platform effort to change how automated analytics workloads authenticate, deploy, and run. The goal is team-owned automation instead of jobs that depend on one person’s credentials.

01 · The challenge

What happens to the pipeline when its owner leaves?

Personal access tokens and user-owned scheduled jobs are an operational risk. When someone’s permissions change or they leave, the automation tied to them can quietly break.

02 · What I did

What I owned and built.

  • Mapped service-principal authentication paths across Azure, Databricks, and source-control workflows.
  • Worked through job and pipeline ownership changes, and identified the limits around immutable creator metadata.
  • Reviewed Git credential and federated identity (OIDC) options for automated deployment and runtime access.
  • Identified secret-rotation and least-privilege considerations for application credentials.

04 · Key decisions

The judgment behind the build.

Why federated identity over stored secrets?

Short-lived tokens issued at run time remove a whole class of leaked or expired secrets.

Why map constraints before migrating?

Some metadata, like a job’s creator, can’t be changed. Knowing that up front avoids breaking production jobs mid-migration.

Why least privilege?

A service identity that can do everything is just a shared password with extra steps. Scoping access limits the blast radius.
